# EXP-009 Result - Emotion Adjective Stack vs Precise Emotional Direction

**Run:** `EXP-009-1.1.1-20260914T022659Z-56XM`  
**Experiment version:** `1.1.1`  
**Benchmark layer:** Transfer Bench  
**Archetype:** `TB-A02` Narrative Restoration Studio Portrait  
**Model target:** `gpt-image-2`  
**Model snapshot:** `unknown`  
**Replicates:** 4 per variant  
**Review protocol:** second-layer blind review with precommitted mapping  
**Evidence classification:** **weakly_contradicts**

## Hypothesis

> One precise emotional direction produces more coherent visible emotional specificity than a stack of loosely overlapping adjectives when all scene facts are held constant.

## Experimental contrast

The control retained the emotion adjective stack:

> `Overall mood: quiet, tense, expectant, uncertain, restrained.`

The variant replaced only that clause with one coherent emotional direction:

> `Overall mood: restrained anticipation, with quiet tension and uncertainty.`

The adult museum conservator, restoration studio, ceramic fragment and padded tray, visible hands, task/window lighting, conservation tools and archival materials, layered composition, observational portrait treatment, aspect ratio, and host generation settings were held constant.

## Interpretation

The precise-emotional-direction condition did not improve the primary `emotional_specificity` dimension. It scored **0.25 lower** than the adjective-stack control, while semantic compliance, aesthetic quality, and technical-defect cleanliness were identical at **5.00** in both conditions.

The overall difference was also small at **-0.0625**. The run is therefore classified **weakly_contradicts**, not `contradicts`.

This result does not establish that adjective stacks are generally superior. It shows that, in the `TB-A02` restoration-studio context, replacing a compatible emotion stack with a concise coherent emotional line did not produce stronger visible emotional specificity.

## Pattern implication

`prompt-structure-then-emotion` moves from `observed` to `mixed`.

The internal corpus observation remains useful evidence that the mechanism occurs in strong prompts, but EXP-009 supplies controlled evidence against treating the precise-emotional-line preference as an unqualified rule.

The pattern was already non-compiler-eligible, so this result does **not** change compiler eligibility or Director-gate behavior.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per condition are insufficient for a statistical-significance claim.
- Only one frozen blind-review score set was used, so inter-rater reliability was not measured.
- The exact host model snapshot and reproducible generation seed were unavailable.
- The finding is specific to `TB-A02`.
- The primary-dimension difference was small at 0.25 points.
- The experiment compares two related emotion-wording strategies and does not establish exact semantic equivalence between every emotional term.
