# EXP-014 Result - Implicit vs Explicit Multi-Reference Roles

**Experiment version:** `1.1.1`
**Benchmark layer:** Transfer Bench
**Archetype:** `TB-A07`
**Model target:** `gpt-image-2`
**Model snapshot:** `unknown`
**Replicates:** 4 per variant
**Review protocol:** second-layer blind review with precommitted mapping
**Evidence classification:** **inconclusive**

## Hypothesis

> Explicit identity, outfit, and pose role declarations reduce cross-reference leakage compared with implicit multi-reference use when the same three fixtures are held constant.

## Experimental contrast

The control used all three references implicitly:

> `Use Reference 1, Reference 2, and Reference 3 to create a realistic full-body portrait while preserving the intended identity, outfit, and pose.`

The variant replaced only that multi-reference instruction with explicit role declarations:

> `Use Reference 1 for identity only, Reference 2 for garment construction and palette only, and Reference 3 for joint arrangement and weight distribution only. Do not transfer unrelated attributes across reference roles.`

The same frozen identity, outfit, and pose fixtures were used in the same reference order.

The warm-gray studio, soft side light, full-body framing, natural-anatomy requirement, garment-silhouette requirement, aspect ratio, and host-generation settings were held constant.

The tested factor was therefore only implicit versus explicit reference-role declaration.

## Interpretation

The explicit-reference-role condition did not improve the registered primary `cross_reference_leakage_control` dimension.

Both conditions scored **5.00**, giving an observed primary-dimension difference of **0.00**.

The explicit-role condition did improve `semantic_compliance` from **4.00** to **5.00** and `reference_role_fidelity` from **4.00** to **5.00**. Aesthetic quality and technical cleanliness were unchanged.

The overall mean increased from **4.6000** to **5.0000**, a difference of **+0.4000**.

EXP-014 is classified **inconclusive**.

The primary leakage-control measure was already saturated at the maximum score for every control replicate, leaving no remaining range on the 5-point scale with which to observe the hypothesized reduction in cross-reference leakage.

The secondary improvements are useful evidence that explicit role declarations may make the intended reference responsibilities clearer in this specific three-reference transfer context. They do not establish the stated leakage-reduction hypothesis because that was not the dimension on which the conditions separated.
A zero observed primary-dimension difference under ceiling saturation is also not evidence that implicit and explicit role declarations are equivalent.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per condition are insufficient for a statistical-significance claim.
- Only one frozen blind-review score set was used, so inter-rater reliability was not measured.
- The exact host model snapshot and reproducible generation seed were unavailable.
- The finding is specific to the `TB-A07` three-reference identity, outfit, and pose transfer context.
- The primary `cross_reference_leakage_control` dimension was ceiling-saturated at **5.00** in both conditions.
- The ceiling prevents this run from measuring whether explicit role declarations reduce leakage beyond the already-maximal observed control score.
- A zero observed primary-dimension difference does not establish equivalence between the two prompting approaches.
- All four replicates within each arm received identical score vectors; the resulting sample SD of zero describes this review sample only and should not be interpreted as zero underlying generation variability.
