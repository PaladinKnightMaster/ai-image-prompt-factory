## Interpretation

In this satin-portrait prompt context, explicitly describing smooth drape, tension folds, and geometry-following specular reflections did not improve perceived material physics over naming satin alone. The explicit variant scored 0.75 lower on the primary `material_physics` dimension and 0.125 lower overall, even though aesthetic quality improved by 0.25.

This result should **not** be interpreted as proof that explicit material-physics language is generally harmful. With only four outputs per variant and one frozen review set, the appropriate conclusion is that this run **contradicts** the tested superiority hypothesis in this context. Replication across other prompts, materials, lighting conditions, and routes is required before drawing a general compiler conclusion.

## Pattern implication

`material-satin-fold-response` should remain non-compiler-eligible. Its evidence state is changed from a corpus-only observation to **mixed**: corpus evidence suggests a plausible mechanism, while the first controlled blinded EXP-002 run did not confirm a benefit and instead moved the primary score in the opposite direction.

Do not promote the pattern to a validated compiler rule from this run.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per variant; no statistical-significance claim is warranted.
- One frozen review score set; inter-rater reliability was not measured.
- Semantic compliance and technical-defect scores were at the ceiling for every image.
- The host exposed `gpt-image-2` but no exact model snapshot.
- The result is specific to satin in this portrait-and-window-light context and requires replication before generalization.
