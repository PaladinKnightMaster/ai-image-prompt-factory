## Interpretation

In this ceramic-vase portrait context, explicit hand-object mechanics improved the primary `hand_object_interaction` score by 0.25 over generic two-hand holding. The treatment also improved semantic compliance by 0.25, aesthetic quality by 0.25, technical-defect cleanliness by 0.50, and the overall mean by 0.3125. The explicit-mechanics group was also more consistent across the four replicates.

This run therefore **supports** the tested superiority hypothesis in this context. It should not be interpreted as proof of universal superiority: four outputs per condition and one frozen review set are not enough for a statistical-significance claim or broad generalization.

## Pattern implication

`interaction-explicit-prop` should remain `supported`, `compiler_eligible: true`, and `director_gate: true`.

The pattern already had internal-corpus and project-rule support. EXP-004 adds direct controlled blinded evidence that explicit contact and load-bearing instructions can improve visible hand-object interaction in the tested portrait context. This result reinforces the existing compiler rule rather than promoting it to a stronger status.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per variant; no statistical-significance claim is warranted.
- One frozen review score set; inter-rater reliability was not measured.
- Most scores were near the top of the 1-5 scale, limiting discrimination on several dimensions.
- The host exposed `gpt-image-2` but no exact model snapshot or reproducible generation seed.
- The finding is specific to a ceramic-vase portrait context, and stochastic outputs varied in framing and prop presentation.
- Replication across different props, poses, hand visibility, scales, and routes is required before stronger generalization.
