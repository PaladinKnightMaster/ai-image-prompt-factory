# EXP-013 Result - Repeated vs Single Precise Identity Lock

**Run:** `EXP-013-1.1.1-20260915T150730Z-E989`
**Experiment version:** `1.1.1`
**Benchmark layer:** Transfer Bench
**Archetype:** `TB-A06` Identity-Only Reference Transfer
**Model target:** `gpt-image-2`
**Model snapshot:** `unknown`
**Replicates:** 4 per variant
**Review protocol:** second-layer blind review with precommitted mapping
**Evidence classification:** **inconclusive**

## Hypothesis

> A single precise identity-only lock preserves identity at least as well as repeating redundant identity locks when the same reference fixture and target scene are held constant.

## Experimental contrast

The control used the frozen identity-only transfer prompt with redundant
identity-lock statements:

> `Identity lock: use Reference 1 only for recognizable facial identity.`
>
> `Preserve the same recognizable identity throughout. Keep the same person in the final image. Do not change identity.`

The variant retained only the single precise identity-role instruction:

> `Identity lock: use Reference 1 only for recognizable facial identity.`

The same frozen synthetic identity fixture, target scene, wardrobe,
composition request, host-generation settings, and reference role were
held constant.

The reference was identity-only and was not intended to transfer clothing,
pose, camera angle, or background.

## Interpretation

The single precise identity-lock condition and repeated-lock condition
both scored **4.75** on the registered primary
`identity_fidelity` dimension.

The observed primary-dimension difference was therefore **0.00**.

The single-lock condition also scored **+0.25** higher on aesthetic quality
and **+0.50** higher on technical cleanliness, while semantic compliance
was unchanged.

The overall mean increased from **4.6875** to
**4.8750**, a difference of **+0.1875**.

EXP-013 is classified **inconclusive**.

The experiment observed no identity-fidelity loss from replacing repeated
identity-lock wording with one precise identity-role instruction in this
specific transfer context.

However, a tie in the primary metric does not establish statistical
equivalence or non-inferiority. No equivalence/non-inferiority margin was
predeclared, and the experiment used only four replicates per condition.

## Compiler and pattern implication

EXP-013 does not justify a compiler-rule promotion from this run alone.

There is no observed identity-fidelity penalty for the single precise lock
in this context, but the experiment does not establish formal equivalence
between single and repeated identity-lock wording.

No VisualPattern confidence is changed automatically.

No golden benchmark is promoted from this result.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per condition are insufficient for a statistical-significance claim.
- Only one frozen blind-review score set was used.
- The exact host model snapshot and generation seed were unavailable.
- The finding is specific to the `TB-A06` identity fixture and transfer scene.
- No non-inferiority or equivalence margin was predeclared.
- Identity-fidelity scores were concentrated near the top of the scale, limiting sensitivity to small differences.
