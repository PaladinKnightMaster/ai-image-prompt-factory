## Hypothesis

> Explicit foreground, midground, and background role assignment improves spatial depth coherence compared with a generic cinematic-depth instruction.

The primary evaluation dimension was `spatial_depth_coherence`.

## Interpretation

In this refined-interior portrait context, explicit foreground/midground/background role assignment improved the primary `spatial_depth_coherence` score by 0.25 over generic cinematic-depth language. Every explicit-layering output received a 5 on the primary dimension, and the treatment group was more consistent across the four replicates.

However, aesthetic quality decreased by 0.50, technical-defect cleanliness decreased by 0.25, and the overall mean decreased by 0.125. Semantic compliance was unchanged. The result therefore **weakly_supports** the tested spatial-depth hypothesis rather than demonstrating general superiority of the explicit treatment.

## Pattern implication

`composition-layered-depth` should remain `supported`, `compiler_eligible: true`, and `director_gate: true` for its existing routes.

The pattern already had repeated internal-corpus observations plus project-rule and compiler/regression support. EXP-005 adds direct controlled blinded evidence that explicit foreground, midground, and background role assignment can slightly improve spatial-depth coherence over generic cinematic-depth language, but the same treatment did not improve overall image quality in this run. The empirical evidence should therefore be recorded as weakly supportive and context-specific rather than used to strengthen the pattern beyond its current supported state.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per variant; no statistical-significance claim is warranted.
- One frozen review score set; inter-rater reliability was not measured.
- The primary `spatial_depth_coherence` score was near the 1-5 scale ceiling in both conditions.
- The host exposed `gpt-image-2` but no exact model snapshot or reproducible generation seed.
- The finding is specific to one refined-interior portrait scenario with a doorway, window, distant furniture, satin outfit, and ceramic vase.
- Replication across other compositions, environments, subjects, focal lengths, and routes is required before stronger generalization.
