# EXP-006 Result - Camera Brand Present vs Removed

**Run:** `EXP-006-1.0.1-20260911T204019Z-EPEL`  
**Experiment version:** `1.0.1`  
**Model target:** `gpt-image-2`

**Replicates:** 4 per variant  
**Review protocol:** second-layer blind review with precommitted mapping  
**Evidence classification:** **supports**

## Hypothesis

> Removing camera-brand wording does not materially reduce visible photographic rendering quality when portrait perspective and scene structure are already specified.

## Experimental contrast

The control retained:

> `Use an 85mm portrait perspective. Hasselblad camera.`

The variant removed only the camera-brand phrase:

> `Use an 85mm portrait perspective.`

Subject, pose, wardrobe, ceramic vase, environment, lighting, composition, 85mm portrait perspective, quality terms, negative constraints, aspect ratio, and generation settings were held constant.

## Preregistered practical decision rule

EXP-006 is an ablation/non-degradation experiment rather than a normal superiority test.

For the primary `photographic_rendering_quality` dimension:

- variant-minus-control delta **>= -0.25**: supports the practical claim that removing the brand phrase does not materially degrade the result;
- a larger negative change would count against the ablation hypothesis.

This is a small-N project decision rule on a 1-5 scale, **not** a formal statistical non-inferiority analysis.

## Interpretation

Removing `Hasselblad camera` did not reduce the primary visible photographic-rendering score. The no-brand condition was actually **+0.25** higher on `photographic_rendering_quality`, **+0.25** higher on technical-defect cleanliness, and **+0.125** higher overall. Semantic compliance and aesthetic quality were unchanged.

Because the primary delta clears the preregistered practical non-degradation threshold, the run is classified **supports**. Within this controlled portrait context, the camera-brand phrase behaved as low-signal language once the 85mm portrait perspective and scene structure were already specified.

This does **not** establish that camera-brand wording is universally useless. The finding should be replicated on richer editorial, environmental, product, artifact, and corpus-derived transfer prompts.

## Limitations

- Four replicates per condition are insufficient for a statistical-significance or formal non-inferiority claim.
- The `-0.25` threshold is a practical project rule on a coarse integer rating scale, not a confidence-interval-based inferential margin.
- Only one blind review score set was collected.
- The exact host model snapshot and reproducible generation seed were unavailable.
- The result is limited to one refined-interior portrait context and needs transfer-benchmark replication.
