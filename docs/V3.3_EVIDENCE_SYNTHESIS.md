# V3.3 Cross-Experiment Evidence Synthesis

**Scope:** STEP 20A
**Experiments:** EXP-014, EXP-016, EXP-017, EXP-018
**Primary mechanism:** explicit identity/outfit/pose reference-role assignment versus implicit multi-reference control
**Primary dimension:** `cross_reference_leakage_control`

## Decision boundary

This synthesis is an evidence review, not a new experiment.

It does not change pattern confidence, compiler behavior, golden benchmarks, experiment results, or frozen artifacts. No machine-readable synthesis schema or registry entry is introduced by STEP 20A.

## Cross-experiment comparison

| Experiment | Context | Primary result | Final evidence | Main interpretive constraint |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| EXP-014 | earlier reference-role test | not used as a stable effect estimate | inconclusive | ceiling saturation limited discrimination |
| EXP-016 | TB-A08 / A02 adversarial fixtures | +2.50 | inconclusive | strong observed score signal, but generation provenance could not independently establish prompt-plus-reference binding for every sample |
| EXP-017 | TB-A08 / A02 adversarial fixtures | +2.25 | supports | provenance-complete local replication; small N and context-specific |
| EXP-018 | TB-A09 / independent A03 adversarial fixtures | +0.25 | inconclusive | independently constructed fixture realization did not reproduce the strong A02 separation |

The EXP-017 and EXP-018 results should not be averaged into a single effect estimate. They are small-N descriptive runs in different frozen fixture contexts, and their review procedures are not identical.

## 1. Evidence for the explicit reference-role mechanism

The current evidence supports a narrower claim than a general mechanism guarantee.

EXP-016 and EXP-017 produced strong positive descriptive differences in the same TB-A08/A02 context. EXP-017 is the strongest evidence in the sequence because it reproduced the positive direction while closing the local execution-binding limitation that forced EXP-016 to remain experiment-level inconclusive.

EXP-018 does not show that explicit reference roles fail. It shows that the strong positive separation observed in A02 did not transfer strongly enough to the independently constructed A03 realization to cross its preregistered support threshold.

Therefore V3.3 contains credible positive evidence that explicit role assignment can improve cross-reference leakage control, but it does not establish a fixture-invariant effect magnitude.

## 2. Fixture and context dependence

The contrast between EXP-017 (+2.25 in A02) and EXP-018 (+0.25 in A03) makes fixture/context sensitivity a serious hypothesis.

It does not establish a causal fixture interaction by itself. Between-run context also includes differences in review procedure and independently constructed realization details. The correct conclusion is therefore that cross-fixture stability is unresolved, not that fixture dependence has been proven.

A future test should cross multiple independently frozen fixture triplets under one preregistered protocol so the same implicit-versus-explicit treatment is evaluated repeatedly across fixture blocks.

## 3. Provenance quality

EXP-016 contributes useful score-level evidence but remains provenance-limited at the experiment-evidence level.

EXP-017 used the task-commitment plus execution-receipt workflow and locally validated the required prompt/reference/output bindings. That closes the specific local provenance limitation identified in EXP-016, while still not providing provider-signed request metadata.

EXP-018 also had locally verified required bindings and was not forced inconclusive by provenance.

EXP-018 must be described as reviewer-specific blindness. The operator-side treatment mapping had already been revealed before independent scoring, while the reviewer attested that condition assignment was not seen before score freeze and that no outside experiment material was consulted. It must not be described as using the older strict global review-freeze-then-reveal ordering.

## 4. Ceiling and floor effects

EXP-014 is not a reliable stable-effect estimate because ceiling saturation limited its discriminatory value.

In EXP-017, semantic compliance, aesthetic quality, and technical cleanliness were ceiling-saturated in both conditions, but the registered primary leakage-control dimension still separated the conditions.

EXP-018 triggered no ceiling or floor safeguard on the primary outcome. Its +0.25 result therefore remains genuinely inconclusive rather than being explained away by a registered ceiling/floor safeguard.

## 5. What remains unknown

V3.3 does not establish:

- a stable treatment-effect magnitude across independently selected fixture triplets;
- whether the A02 result was unusually favorable, the A03 result unusually weak, or both fall within normal between-fixture heterogeneity;
- which identity, outfit, pose, or conflict-signature properties moderate the treatment effect;
- inter-rater stability across multiple independent reviewers;
- statistical significance or a population-level effect;
- provider-signed generation-input provenance.

## 6. V3.3 evidence decision

The explicit reference-role mechanism has credible positive evidence, strongest in the provenance-complete EXP-017 replication.

Generalization across fixture realizations remains unresolved because EXP-018 did not reproduce the strong A02 separation.

Accordingly:

- no automatic pattern-confidence mutation;
- no compiler mutation;
- no golden promotion;
- no claim of a fixture-invariant mechanism effect;
- no additional wording tweak is justified inside V3.3 merely to recover a stronger score.

This is a sufficient evidence boundary for V3.3 closure after the normal repository closure audit.

## 7. Recommended next scientific question

The next experiment should be deferred to the next research scope rather than used to keep V3.3 open.

**Recommended question for EXP-019:**

> Under one fixed preregistered implicit-versus-explicit reference-role treatment and review protocol, does cross-reference leakage control show a consistent treatment benefit across multiple independently frozen identity/outfit/pose fixture triplets, or does the treatment effect materially vary by fixture realization?

The design should treat fixture triplets as preregistered blocks/context realizations while keeping the explicit-versus-implicit reference-role treatment as the manipulated factor. This directly tests cross-fixture robustness without introducing another prompt-wording variation.
