# V3.4 Transformation Intelligence
## Final Architecture Freeze
### Amendment: Visual Semantic Lexicon + Lexical Realization

**Status:** Architecture frozen — implementation next
**Implementation:** Not started
**Target:** V3.4
**Supersedes:** The previous V3.4 research/architecture brief only where explicitly amended below
**Preserved principle:** TransformationPack remains a representation-change contract and gains no historical, cultural, artifact, or fabrication authority.

---

# 1. Assessment: does lexicalization change the approved architecture?

## Decision

**No fundamental redesign is required.**

The lexicalization insight exposes a missing layer between:

> correct semantic resolution

and

> final prompt wording.

It does not change who decides what is historically correct, what art method applies, what artifact exists, or how a representation transforms.

The repository already contains the correct semantic authorities:

- EraPacks;
- ArtMethodPacks;
- artifact forms/states;
- reference roles;
- identity locks;
- evidence claims;
- compatibility gates;
- Director decisions.

It also already has a natural insertion point. The current compiler loads structured packs and then directly turns their `prompt_fragments`, material behaviors, artifact geometry, state, and Director fields into prose sections.

V3.4 should insert a **controlled lexical realization operation at that seam**.

## Architectural interpretation

The new distinction is:

```text
SEMANTICS
What has been decided?

versus

LEXICALIZATION
How should that already-decided concept be expressed to the image model?
```

That is a legitimate first-class distinction.

The new components therefore are:

### Visual Semantic Lexicon

A reusable, evidence-linked vocabulary of domain concepts and their visible meanings.

### Lexical Realizer

A deterministic compiler substage that expresses **resolved concept IDs** as model-facing visual language.

Neither is a reasoning authority.

## Critical correction to the motivating hypothesis

Do not encode this statement as fact:

> “The domain term activates the concept.”

Instead encode:

> A canonical term preserves domain precision; a visible gloss states the expected visual manifestation; whether their combination improves generation is empirically testable.

This matters because terms such as `contrapposto`, `bokashi`, and `pouncing` have precise but conditional meanings. Contrapposto describes a standing figure whose weight is primarily carried by one leg, with compensating shifts through the body. Bokashi is an intentionally produced printed gradation. Pouncing is historically evidenced at Dunhuang, but it was a transfer method, not a mandatory visual mark on every mural.

The lexicon must therefore never operate like:

```text
more specialist vocabulary
=
better prompt
```

It operates like:

```text
resolved concept
+
canonical terminology
+
visible explanation
+
only the context already authorized
=
precise model-facing realization
```

There are deliberately **two interactions with the lexicon**.

### Before semantic resolution

The lexicon may help recognize ordinary language.

That lookup produces **candidates only**.

### After semantic resolution

The Lexical Realizer receives only concepts that have survived the authoritative semantic and compatibility systems.

This two-stage distinction is essential.

Without it, an alias such as:

```text
"Greek cape"
```

could silently become:

```text
himation
```

without the system determining whether that garment fits the requested date, role, sex/gender presentation, activity, region, or desired reconstruction mode.

That would turn a vocabulary system into an unreviewed historical inference engine.

# 3. Exact responsibility of the Visual Semantic Lexicon

## It owns

The lexicon owns the **linguistic and visible identity of reusable concepts**.

For each concept it may define:

```text
canonical domain term
ordinary-language aliases
broad retrieval phrases
visible gloss
atomic visual signatures
realizations that should be avoided
non-authoritative retrieval scope
links to canonical evidence records
```

Example:

```text
concept:
  woodblock.bokashi

canonical term:
  bokashi

visible gloss:
  a controlled printed gradation that transitions within a color region

visual signatures:
  - localized gradual change in pigment density
  - remains part of block-print color structure
  - does not turn the whole image into continuous photographic shading
```

The British Museum describes bokashi as graduated color produced with water, pigment, and brushing on the block, while the existing repository already treats it as optional rather than mandatory.

## It does not own

The lexicon does **not** decide:

- whether Edo is the correct period;
- whether bokashi belongs in a particular print;
- whether a figure should wear a himation;
- whether contrapposto applies;
- whether a Dunhuang case may use pouncing;
- whether the requested ceramic is Chinese, Japanese, Persian, Dutch, or another tradition;
- whether a vase should use a specific decorative motif;
- whether identity should be strict or abstracted;
- what TransformationPack applies;
- whether a historical claim is canonical or interpretive.

Those remain external decisions.

## It is not a synonym dictionary

A conventional thesaurus says:

```text
cloak
≈
mantle
≈
cape
≈
himation
```

That is inadequate.

A Visual Semantic Lexicon says:

```text
himation
=
specific canonical concept

visible behavior:
large rectangular mantle/cloak,
draped or wrapped around the body

historical applicability:
resolved elsewhere

ordinary phrase:
"Greek cape"
may RETRIEVE this candidate
but cannot establish it as correct
```

## It is not a keyword library

The lexicon should never be iterated like:

```text
for concept in greek_terms:
    prompt += concept
```

The 20–30 MVP entries are a **candidate knowledge pool**, not a prompt checklist.

For example:

- `contrapposto` should not appear in a portrait bust that has no meaningful leg stance;
- `peplos`, `chiton`, and `himation` should not all appear merely because they are Greek terms;
- `bokashi` should not appear if no gradation has been resolved;
- `pouncing` should not be added as generic “Dunhuang texture”;
- polychromy should not become automatic bright paint on every marble artifact.

# 4. Exact responsibility of the Lexical Realizer

## Definition

The Lexical Realizer is a **deterministic compiler-stage operation** that converts:

```text
resolved concept IDs
+
resolved semantic context
+
realization profile
+
prompt budget
+
model-adapter presentation constraints
```

into:

```text
model-facing visual language
+
trace metadata
```

## Hard prohibition

It must not:

```text
search for new facts
infer a new era
select a new garment
select an art method
resolve compatibility
change identity policy
choose a transformation
invent a material
infer an artifact form
add a culturally specific ornament
upgrade an uncertain historical claim
```

If required context is absent, the realizer **backs off**.

It does not fill the missing fact creatively.

For example:

Resolved:

```text
concept = garment.himation
material = unresolved
```

Allowed:

```text
a himation, a large rectangular cloak wrapped around the body
```

Not allowed:

```text
a heavy wool himation...
```

unless wool has already been resolved by the authoritative semantic/evidence layer.

## Input contract

Conceptually:

```text
ResolvedConcept:
  concept_id
  source_component
  resolved_modifiers
  evidence_refs
  compatibility_status
```

Example:

```text
concept_id:
  garment.himation

source_component:
  era.classical_greek.wardrobe

resolved_modifiers:
  material: wool
  placement: wrapped diagonally around lower body

compatibility_status:
  well_attested
```

## Output contract

```text
LexicalRealization:
  concept_id
  preferred_term
  profile
  realized_text
  source_component
  evidence_refs
  omitted_signature_atoms
```

Example:

```text
a wool himation wrapped around the lower body in broad layered folds
```

## No hidden generative reasoning

For V3.4, the Lexical Realizer should **not itself be an LLM call**.

That would defeat:

- deterministic compilation;
- regression hashes;
- traceability;
- controlled experiments;
- evidence provenance.

It should operate from structured data and resolved modifiers.

Future research could test model-assisted lexicalization separately, but it does not belong in this architecture freeze.

# 5. Proposed minimum Visual Semantic Lexicon schema

## Decision

**Yes, this warrants one first-class schema.**

Without a schema, this layer would rapidly decay into miscellaneous prose fragments.

But the schema should be much smaller than the candidate design suggested.

In particular, V3.4 should **not store separate compact, standard, strict, and technical prompt sentences for every concept**.

That would recreate the template duplication problem at a smaller scale.

## Proposed schema

Conceptually:

```text
VisualSemanticConcept

id
version

preferred_term
domain

aliases[]
retrieval_terms[]

visible_gloss
visual_signature[]

avoid_realizations[]

retrieval_scope
  pack_ids[]
  subperiods[]
  regions[]
  roles[]
  occasions[]
  art_method_ids[]
  artifact_form_ids[]

evidence_refs
  claim_ids[]
  source_ids[]

coalesce_group
```

### Required fields

Require:

```text
id
version
preferred_term
domain
visible_gloss
evidence_refs
```

At least one of:

```text
claim_ids
source_ids
```

must be present.

### Concept identity and term validation

Concept IDs must be globally unique and stable; duplicate concept IDs are invalid. `preferred_term` does not need to be globally unique forever. Two distinct semantic concepts may share a human-readable preferred term when they represent genuinely different senses or domains, provided they have different stable IDs and are sufficiently disambiguated by their domain and visible meaning. Aliases must not silently collapse different semantic concepts; ambiguous term or alias lookup produces candidates for semantic resolution, never an automatic merge or selection.

### `aliases`

Use only for genuine alternate labels, transliterations, spelling forms, or near-equivalent nomenclature.

Example:

```text
preferred_term:
  kentō registration

aliases:
  - kento registration
  - kentō marks
```

### `retrieval_terms`

Broader ordinary-language phrases that can surface the concept as a candidate.

Example:

```text
retrieval_terms:
  - Japanese print alignment
  - lining up color blocks
  - old Japanese print registration
```

This distinction is important.

An alias can imply:

```text
likely same lexical concept
```

A retrieval term means only:

```text
possibly relevant concept
```

## `visible_gloss`

One concise explanation of what should be visibly understood.

Example:

```text
preferred_term:
  contrapposto

visible_gloss:
  standing pose with most body weight carried by one leg and the other leg relaxed or bent, producing compensating shifts through the hips and upper body
```

This is consistent with museum descriptions of the pose.

## `visual_signature`

A small list of atomic cues.

Not whole prompt templates.

Example:

```text
visual_signature:
  - primary weight-bearing leg
  - relaxed or bent opposite leg
  - asymmetrical hip balance
```

Profiles may select among these atoms.

## `avoid_realizations`

Concept-specific semantic anti-patterns.

Example:

```text
woodblock.bokashi

avoid_realizations:
  - photographic global gradient shading
  - airbrushed digital gradient
```

This is preferable to putting long negative prompts inside every case.

## `retrieval_scope`

Rename the candidate field `scope` to:

```text
retrieval_scope
```

because `scope` sounds authoritative.

This field is an indexing/relevance hint.

It **must not define historical compatibility**.

Historical compatibility remains in evidence claims and gates. Existing evidence claims already represent pack, subperiod, region, social role, occasion, art-method, compatibility, confidence, and source IDs.

## `evidence_refs`

Do not duplicate:

```text
URL
institution
publication date
confidence
compatibility
```

inside every lexicon record.

The repository already has an evidence-source registry and evidence claims with source IDs, context, compatibility, and numeric confidence.

Therefore:

```text
lexicon concept
→ claim_id / source_id
→ canonical evidence subsystem
```

not:

```text
lexicon concept
→ copied museum citation metadata
```

## `coalesce_group`

Optional.

Used to allow several compatible concepts to become one concise clause.

Example:

```text
woodblock.keyblock
woodblock.color_block
woodblock.kento_registration
woodblock.baren_printing

coalesce_group:
  woodblock.print_production
```

This avoids producing four robotic sentences.

## Domain enum

The schema should be capable of future reuse across:

```text
garment
hairstyle_headwear
makeup_grooming
jewelry_accessory
architecture
furniture
object_tool
food_tableware
weapon
transportation
pose_composition
material
craft_process
art_method
artifact_form
environment
lighting_technology
representation_detail
```

Only a small subset needs records in V3.4.

# 6. Integration with existing knowledge systems

The core source-of-truth rule is:

```text
PACKS / EVIDENCE
own semantic truth

LEXICON
owns concept expression

REALIZER
owns final wording
```

## EraPack

EraPack determines:

```text
what belongs
where
when
for whom
under what reconstruction mode
```

It may reference a lexicon concept ID after resolution.

Example:

```text
classical_greek
→ compatible wardrobe decision
→ garment.himation
```

The lexicon then explains what a himation should visibly mean.

The Met describes chiton, peplos, and himation as distinct structural garment categories, including the peplos's defining overfold and shoulder fastening and the himation as a large rectangular cloak.

The lexicon does not decide which garment the subject wears.

## ArtMethodPack

ArtMethodPack owns fabrication.

It exposes resolved concept IDs such as:

```text
method.japanese_woodblock_print
woodblock.keyblock
woodblock.color_block
woodblock.kento_registration
woodblock.baren_printing
```

and conditionally:

```text
woodblock.bokashi
```

The existing woodblock pack already separates key/color blocks, baren, registration, optional bokashi, paper behavior, and print-specific failures.

V3.4 should stop requiring the compiler to repeatedly restate that knowledge through handcrafted method-specific prose.

## Artifact Form

Artifact Form owns geometry.

For example:

```text
vase
→ neck
→ shoulder
→ belly
→ rotational surface
```

The lexicon may express an already-resolved concept such as:

```text
ceramic.vessel_zones
```

but does not invent the vase geometry.

## Artifact State

State does not select lexical terminology except to provide already-resolved modifiers.

For example:

```text
museum_conserved
```

may authorize a realization that acknowledges stabilized losses.

It may not cause:

```text
cracked
aged
weathered
antique
```

to be sprayed through the lexical layer automatically.

## TransformationPack

No amendment.

TransformationPack may request:

```text
surface_map_geometry
```

but it does not know:

```text
cobalt underglaze
kentō
himation
bokashi
```

## Wardrobe / hair / environment / objects

These knowledge areas may progressively reference lexicon IDs.

Do not migrate every current text record during V3.4.

The schema is intentionally broad enough for future expansion, but V3.4 only proves the system with the four transformation families.

## Existing `prompt_fragments`

This is the awkward migration boundary.

The repository currently contains substantial model-facing prose directly inside EraPack and ArtMethodPack `prompt_fragments`. The compiler selects those fragments directly.

Do not attempt a repository-wide migration in V3.4.

Freeze this rule instead:

### Legacy path

Existing cases may continue using existing `prompt_fragments` unchanged.

### V3.4 lexicalized path

For the new V3.4 transformation cases:

```text
resolved concepts
→ lexicon
→ lexical realizer
```

must be used for concepts covered by the V3.4 lexicon.

The compiler must not emit both:

```text
old prose explaining bokashi
+
new lexicalized bokashi clause
```

in the same prompt.

### Migration rule

Once a semantic concept has a lexicon record, **new implementation work should reference the concept ID rather than create another independent prose definition**.

Full migration can occur in a later release.

# 7. Three realization levels and Prompt Realization Profiles

There are really **three semantic realization levels** and **four operational profiles**.

They should not be confused.

## Level 1 — Canonical term

Stored.

Example:

```text
himation
```

Purpose:

- domain precision;
- traceability;
- user/expert recognition;
- stable concept ID mapping.

## Level 2 — Visible gloss

Stored.

Example:

```text
a large rectangular cloak wrapped or draped around the body
```

Purpose:

- make the concept visually explicit;
- avoid reliance on obscure jargon;
- provide a stable model-facing semantic anchor.

## Level 3 — Scene-specific realization

Generated deterministically.

Example:

```text
a wool himation wrapped diagonally around the lower body in broad layered folds over the finer chiton folds
```

This may only use already-resolved facts:

```text
wool
lower-body placement
chiton layering
fold relationship
```

The Late Classical marble woman at the Met, for example, visibly distinguishes crinkly linen chiton folds from the heavier wool himation.

That does **not** authorize the system to assume that exact material combination for every Greek request.

---

## Prompt Realization Profiles

Keep these separate from the existing:

```text
output.profile:
  compact
  standard
  extended
```

The current schema uses those values for overall prompt shape/verbosity.

Add:

```text
output.lexical_profile
```

with:

```text
auto
compact
descriptive
historical_precision
artifact_technical
```

### `compact`

Output:

```text
canonical term
+
minimal visible gloss
```

No optional signature atoms unless needed to prevent a known failure.

Example:

```text
a himation, a large rectangular wrapped cloak
```

### `descriptive`

Output:

```text
canonical term
+
visible gloss
+
one or two applicable visible cues
```

Example:

```text
a himation, a large rectangular mantle wrapped around the body in broad layered folds
```

### `historical_precision`

This lexical realization profile expresses already-resolved historically relevant concepts with enough discriminative detail to preserve their meaning. It performs no historical reasoning, compatibility resolution, evidence upgrading, or semantic selection. Only qualifiers already accepted by the evidence/compatibility layer may enter. No speculative embellishment.

### `artifact_technical`

Prioritize:

- layer relationships;
- fabrication-visible effects;
- surface behavior;
- process-defining details;
- artifact integration cues;
- known method failure prevention.

Example:

```text
brush-painted cobalt decoration beneath a transparent glaze on a white porcelain body, with exposed ceramic ground between motifs and decoration following the vessel curvature
```

The V&A defines blue-and-white broadly as cobalt decoration on a white body, typically brush-applied under glaze, while the existing ArtMethodPack separately encodes curvature, reserve white, glaze/body layering, and vessel zones.

### `auto`

Determine lexical detail from:

```text
existing output profile
historical strictness
route
TransformationPack
art-method complexity
known method failures
available prompt budget
model adapter
```

Suggested decision order:

```text
if explicit lexical_profile:
    use it

else if artifact/art-method transformation has critical fabrication failures:
    artifact_technical

else if strict_reconstruction:
    historical_precision

else if output.profile == compact:
    compact

else:
    descriptive
```

Historical strictness always remains independently authoritative.

An `artifact_technical` profile cannot bypass strict historical constraints.

## Budget policy

When shortening prompts, the realizer should reduce:

```text
optional signature atoms
secondary explanation
redundant modifiers
```

before dropping the meaning of a required resolved concept.

Do not turn:

```text
kentō registration, using carved alignment notches to place each color impression consistently
```

into merely:

```text
kentō
```

just because the prompt is compact.

## Model adapter policy

Do not maintain:

```text
GPT Image lexicon
Model X lexicon
Model Y lexicon
```

The concepts remain model-independent.

A model adapter may influence:

```text
clause length
ordering
punctuation
detail budget
```

but not domain truth.

If later experiments prove a model-specific realization advantage, that evidence belongs in:

```text
adapter policy
or
VisualPattern / empirical mechanism data
```

not a duplicated vocabulary database.

# 8. Ordinary-user phrase and alias resolution

The input path should be:

```text
user wording
→ lexical candidates
→ semantic / historical / method resolution
→ compatibility gates
→ resolved concept IDs
→ lexical realization
```

Never:

```text
user wording
→ alias match
→ canonical concept treated as historical truth
```

## Example A

User:

```text
Greek cape
```

Candidate retrieval:

```text
garment.himation
```

Then EraPack/gates determine whether that candidate is compatible.

Only after acceptance does the prompt receive:

```text
a himation, a large rectangular cloak wrapped around the body...
```

## Example B

User:

```text
old Japanese print
```

Candidate retrieval may include:

```text
method.japanese_woodblock_print
```

It does not automatically conclude:

```text
Edo
ukiyo-e
Hiroshige
bokashi
```

Those are separate decisions.

## Example C

User:

```text
blue pottery
```

Candidate retrieval may include:

```text
method.blue_white_ceramic
ceramic.cobalt_pigment
```

but the system cannot infer:

```text
Ming
Jingdezhen
Arita
Edo
```

from that phrase alone.

The existing ceramic pack explicitly warns that not every blue-and-white tradition should be treated as Ming Jingdezhen.

## Example D

User:

```text
ancient wall painting
```

Possible candidate:

```text
method.mineral_pigment_mural
```

That does not establish:

```text
Dunhuang
Mogao
Tang
Cave 85
```

Those require historical resolution.

## Matching mechanism for V3.4

Keep candidate lookup simple:

```text
exact preferred-term match
→ aliases
→ normalized phrase retrieval_terms
```

No embeddings, ontology graph, fuzzy knowledge inference, or LLM classifier is required for the V3.4 MVP.

Candidate output should record:

```text
candidate concept ID
matched phrase
match type
```

but not:

```text
historical confidence
compatibility verdict
```

Those belong elsewhere.

# 9. Initial 30-concept V3.4 lexicon scope

These 30 concepts are enough to test the architecture.

They are **not all injected into each prompt**.

## A. Classical Greek / marble — 8 concepts

Museum evidence distinguishes the Greek chiton, peplos, and himation structurally; Classical sculpture provides a defensible visible definition of contrapposto; and surviving pigment research demonstrates that ancient Greek marble should not be assumed to have been uniformly bare white.

| ID | Preferred term | Minimum visible gloss |
|---|---|---|
| `method.marble_sculpture` | marble sculpture | human form physically translated into carved stone mass and surface rather than stone-colored living skin |
| `garment.chiton` | chiton | rectangular tunic construction fastened or joined at the shoulders and shaped by girdling/drape |
| `garment.peplos` | peplos | rectangular draped garment with a characteristic upper overfold and shoulder fastening |
| `garment.himation` | himation | large rectangular cloak or mantle wrapped or draped around the body |
| `pose.contrapposto` | contrapposto | standing weight carried primarily by one leg, with the other relaxed and compensating asymmetry through the body |
| `sculpture.carved_drapery` | carved drapery | garment folds translated into stone ridges, troughs, overlaps, and weight-bearing carved masses |
| `sculpture.sculptural_hair_masses` | sculptural hair masses | hair simplified into stone-continuous locks and volumes rather than floating individual strands |
| `sculpture.polychromy` | polychromy | evidence-conditioned applied color or surviving pigment on sculpture rather than an automatic pure-white-marble assumption |

`contrapposto` is intentionally in the vocabulary even though it should normally be irrelevant to the bust MVP case.

That is a useful architectural test: **available concept does not equal emitted concept**.

---

## B. Dunhuang / mineral-pigment mural — 7 concepts

Getty conservation work at Mogao describes walls prepared with clay, sand, and plant fiber, a ground layer, preliminary line drawing, and mineral pigments. The Met separately documents pouncing as a transfer technique used by Dunhuang mural artists by the tenth century.

| ID | Preferred term | Minimum visible gloss |
|---|---|---|
| `method.mineral_pigment_mural` | mineral-pigment mural | painting materially bound to a prepared wall surface using drawn contours and pigment layers rather than a framed digital painting |
| `mural.prepared_earthen_plaster` | prepared earthen plaster | clay/sand/fiber-based wall preparation forming the physical support beneath the painting |
| `mural.ground_layer` | ground layer | prepared lighter painting ground applied above the earthen plaster before the painted design |
| `mural.line_drawing` | line drawing | drawn contour structure defining figures and forms before or alongside the color fields |
| `mural.mineral_pigment_fields` | mineral-pigment color fields | matte pigment regions integrated with the wall rather than luminous digital gradients |
| `mural.wall_integration` | wall-integrated painting | painted forms physically share the grain, cracks, losses, and planar architecture of the wall |
| `mural.pouncing_transfer` | pouncing transfer | evidence-conditioned transfer of a preparatory design through repeated points or perforated-pattern transfer, not a mandatory decorative texture |

The existing ArtMethodPack already correctly states that pouncing traces should appear only when evidence supports them.

---

## C. Edo Japanese woodblock — 7 concepts

The Met documents carved relief blocks, separate blocks for colors, registration, and mulberry-paper support. The British Museum provides explicit technical terminology for the keyline block, kentō registration, baren printing, and bokashi gradation.

| ID | Preferred term | Minimum visible gloss |
|---|---|---|
| `method.japanese_woodblock_print` | Japanese woodblock print | relief-printed image built from carved contours and separately printed color regions on paper |
| `woodblock.keyblock` | keyblock | principal carved block carrying the defining contour/key lines of the design |
| `woodblock.color_block` | color block | separate carved block used to print a particular color area in registration with the design |
| `woodblock.kento_registration` | kentō registration | carved alignment notches that position the sheet consistently across successive color impressions |
| `woodblock.baren_printing` | baren printing | hand pressure applied by rubbing the back of the paper to transfer pigment from the carved block |
| `woodblock.bokashi` | bokashi | controlled printed gradation within selected color regions rather than global photographic shading |
| `woodblock.kozo_paper` | kōzo paper | resilient paper-mulberry-fiber support capable of receiving repeated hand-printed impressions |

The current repository already models precisely these method behaviors, including the conditional nature of bokashi.

---

## D. Blue-and-white ceramic — 8 concepts

Blue-and-white ceramic is materially based on cobalt decoration on a light ceramic body, usually applied beneath glaze; the existing repository additionally models brush density, glaze/body layering, reserve white, vessel zones, and curvature-aware decoration.

| ID | Preferred term | Minimum visible gloss |

# 10. Empirical hypothesis and test strategy

## Hypothesis name

**Domain-Grounded Lexical Realization**

## Hypothesis

For historically or art-technically constrained image generation:

> canonical domain terminology combined with a visible gloss may improve the appropriate semantic, historical/cultural, art/material, or transformation fidelity relative to generic everyday description, without materially degrading aesthetic quality.

This is a **hypothesis**, not a V3.4 compiler claim.

## Better experimental design

Use three conditions.

### Control C — generic visible language

Accurate everyday description with no specialist term.

Example:

```text
Create a controlled printed gradient that changes gradually from darker pigment to lighter pigment inside selected flat color regions.
```

### Variant A — canonical term only

```text
Use restrained bokashi.
```

### Variant B — canonical term + identical visible semantics

```text
Use restrained bokashi, a controlled printed gradation from darker to lighter pigment within selected color regions.
```

This design is stronger than simply comparing:

```text
generic
vs
technical+gloss
```

because:

### B vs C

tests the incremental contribution of the canonical term while holding the visible concept substantially constant.

### B vs A

tests the contribution of the visible explanation relative to jargon alone.

Variant A is especially important.

If:

```text
technical term only
```

performs no better or performs worse, the project avoids turning expert vocabulary into a cargo-cult prompt mechanism.

## Do not artificially length-match

Do not add meaningless filler merely to equalize word count.

Instead preregister and report:

```text
character count
word count
token estimate if available
```

as a limitation/confound.

## First diagnostic target

A strong first lexical mechanism is:

```text
woodblock.bokashi
```

or:

```text
ceramic.underglaze_decoration
```

because both have:

- concrete physical meaning;
- visible consequences;
- clear failure alternatives;
- museum-backed terminology.

## Transfer requirement

Do not promote the lexical mechanism globally from one family.

After the first diagnostic experiment, test equivalent C/A/B contrasts across:

```text
Greek garment/sculpture
Dunhuang mural
Edo print
ceramic artifact
```

V3.3 already established why this matters: prompt mechanisms can be context/fixture sensitive.

## Scoring

Do not make one global primary score for all four families.

Use a family-appropriate primary dimension.

### Greek garment / sculpture

Primary:

```text
historical_cultural_integrity
```

or, for carved representation terminology:

```text
art_material_fidelity
```

### Dunhuang

Primary:

```text
art_material_fidelity
```

### Woodblock

Primary:

```text
art_material_fidelity
```

### Ceramic

Primary:

```text
art_material_fidelity
```

Common secondary dimensions:

```text
semantic_compliance
transformation_fidelity
aesthetic_quality
technical_defects
```

The existing evaluation schema already separates semantic, aesthetic, historical/cultural, art/material, identity, and technical scores and permits additional dimensions.

## New diagnostic failure

Add:

```text
TERM_REALIZATION_FAILURE
```

Definition:

> The correct technical concept was present in the compiled prompt, but the expected visible structure/effect associated with that concept is materially absent or incorrectly realized in the image.

Examples:

```text
"bokashi" present
but image contains smooth global digital shading

"underglaze" present
but blue decoration floats above glaze like vinyl

"contrapposto" present
but figure stands symmetrically on both legs

"himation" present
but rendered as a fitted modern cape
```

## Do not add a taxonomy explosion

Use existing transformation/art failures when they are more precise.

For example:

```text
SURFACE_MAPPING_FAILURE
```

is still the correct failure for a flat portrait pasted onto a vase.

`TERM_REALIZATION_FAILURE` answers a narrower research question:

> Did the expected visual meaning of the stated technical term materialize?

Similarly, keyword stuffing should continue to be treated as an over-constraint/prompt-quality problem rather than creating:

```text
TOO_MANY_TECHNICAL_TERMS
```

as a new permanent class.

## Infrastructure

Reuse the V3.3 empirical system:

```text
preregistration
frozen prompt variants
frozen reference fixtures
independent generation
execution receipts
blind review
freeze before reveal
result provenance
transfer testing
```

The existing experiment schema already supports multiple prompt variants and arbitrary registered evaluation dimensions, so this hypothesis does not justify another experiment framework.

# New criteria 49–63

## Lexicon integrity

**49.** V3.4 contains one first-class Visual Semantic Lexicon schema and one MVP registry/collection containing approximately 20–30 concepts; no era-specific expression-library hierarchy is created.

**50.** Every compiler-eligible lexical concept contains at least a stable ID, preferred term, domain, visible gloss, and canonical evidence reference. A specialist term without a visible gloss is invalid.

Concept IDs must be globally unique; duplicate IDs are invalid. Distinct concepts may share a `preferred_term` only when they have different stable IDs and sufficiently disambiguated senses/domains. Aliases must not silently merge those concepts.

**51.** `aliases` and `retrieval_terms` have separate semantics: aliases represent lexical equivalents or close nomenclature, while broad ordinary-language retrieval terms may only produce candidates.

**52.** `retrieval_scope` is explicitly non-authoritative and cannot override evidence claims, historical strictness, EraPack, ArtMethodPack, artifact compatibility, or gates.

**53.** Lexicon provenance references the repository's canonical evidence claims/source registry. Lexicon files must not create a second independent bibliography, confidence system, or compatibility database.

**54.** EraPack, ArtMethodPack, Artifact Form, and other semantic knowledge refer to lexicon concept IDs rather than duplicating new independent definitions of the same term. Existing legacy prose may remain temporarily for backward compatibility.

## Realizer authority

**55.** The Lexical Realizer accepts only resolved concept IDs and already-resolved contextual modifiers for final realization.

**56.** The Lexical Realizer performs no new historical, cultural, role, art-method, transformation, artifact-form, state, material, or compatibility inference.

**57.** If a context-specific modifier is not resolved, the realizer falls back to the canonical term plus visible gloss rather than inventing the missing modifier.

## Profile behavior

**58.** Prompt realization profiles modify lexical detail, selection of already-authorized signature atoms, and phrasing density only; they cannot change semantic truth or compatibility decisions.

**59.** The compact lexical profile preserves the visible meaning of required resolved concepts while dropping optional details before dropping semantic constraints.

**60.** Profiles do not store separate complete prompt versions. One lexicon concept definition must support all realization profiles.

## Traceability and anti-stuffing

**61.** Every emitted lexical concept is represented in `lexical_trace` with concept ID, source component, realization profile, realized text, and evidence reference.

**62.** The compiler emits only the resolved subset of the lexicon. It must never dump all concepts associated with an era or art method into the prompt; concept availability is not concept selection.

## Empirical discipline

**63.** The Domain-Grounded Lexical Realization hypothesis is preregistered and tested through V3.3 empirical infrastructure before any claim that `canonical term + visible gloss` is generally superior. `TERM_REALIZATION_FAILURE` is tracked diagnostically, and no lexical mechanism receives global promotion from one successful family or fixture.

Do not create independent Era-specific expression libraries.

### Law 7 — evidence remains centralized

Lexicon concepts reference evidence; they do not create a second historical evidence model.

### Law 8 — profiles control detail, not truth

`artifact_technical` and `historical_precision` affect how much already-authorized detail appears.

### Law 9 — legacy behavior remains protected

V3.4 proves the lexical layer on the four new transformation families before any repository-wide prose migration.

### Law 10 — lexical effectiveness remains empirical

The architecture may support:

```text
canonical term
+
visible gloss
```

but only controlled V3.3-style evidence may justify making it a generally preferred prompt mechanism.

# Final freeze decision

**Approve V3.4 with this amendment.**

The new lexical layer solves a real missing problem without disturbing the approved TransformationPack architecture.

The final architectural distinction is now clean:

```text
TransformationPack
knows HOW REPRESENTATION CHANGES.

EraPack / ArtMethodPack / Artifact knowledge
know WHAT THE RESOLVED DOMAIN CONCEPT IS.

Visual Semantic Lexicon
knows WHAT THAT CONCEPT IS CALLED AND WHAT IT SHOULD LOOK LIKE.

Lexical Realizer
knows HOW MUCH OF THAT ALREADY-RESOLVED MEANING TO EXPRESS.

Compiler
puts the final production instruction together.
```

That is the correct freeze boundary.

The dangerous alternative would be to make the lexicon “smart” enough to choose historical facts. That would quietly create a second Temporal-Cultural Engine inside the compiler and undo the separation achieved by the rest of V3.4.

The second dangerous alternative would be to treat the 30 expert terms as a quality vocabulary and inject them whenever an associated era or method appears. That would simply replace generic keyword stuffing with specialist keyword stuffing.

V3.4 should implement neither.
